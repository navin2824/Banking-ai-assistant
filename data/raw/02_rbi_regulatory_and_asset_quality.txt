RBI REGULATORY KNOWLEDGE — BANKING ASSET QUALITY Document type:
Educational summary with official-source references Jurisdiction: India
Prepared: 11 November 2026

PURPOSE AND SCOPE This document introduces the role of the Reserve Bank
of India and several prudential banking concepts. It is intended for
domain-specific AI testing. It is not a complete statement of law or
regulation. Regulatory requirements can change and may differ across
banks, cooperative banks, non-banking financial companies and other
regulated entities.

1.  ROLE OF THE RESERVE BANK OF INDIA The Reserve Bank of India, or RBI,
    is India’s central bank. It performs monetary, regulatory and
    supervisory functions in the financial system. Its responsibilities
    include monetary policy, currency management, regulation and
    supervision of specified financial institutions, payment-system
    oversight and financial-stability work. Banking entities must follow
    the laws, regulations, directions and circulars applicable to their
    entity type and activities.

2.  PRUDENTIAL REGULATION Prudential rules are intended to promote the
    safety and soundness of regulated financial institutions. They
    address areas such as governance, capital, liquidity, credit
    exposure, asset classification, income recognition, provisioning,
    disclosures and risk management. The exact requirements depend on
    the regulated entity and the current applicable instrument. A
    requirement for one class of institution should not automatically be
    applied to another.

3.  INCOME RECOGNITION Income recognition rules govern when an
    institution may recognise income, including interest on advances.
    Recognition may depend on the status and classification of an
    account and the applicable prudential framework. Accounting
    recognition and cash actually received are not always the same
    thing. A reliable answer should identify the relevant rule and
    account category rather than assuming that every accrued amount is
    automatically recognised as income.

4.  ASSET CLASSIFICATION Asset classification categorises advances
    according to the applicable prudential criteria and the repayment or
    credit status of the account. Categories and conditions are defined
    by the relevant regulatory directions. Classification can depend on
    the nature of the facility, account conduct, restructuring status
    and other specified facts. The current entity-specific rule must be
    checked before stating an exact overdue period or classification
    threshold.

5.  NON-PERFORMING ASSETS A non-performing asset, or NPA, is an advance
    classified as non-performing under the applicable RBI prudential
    framework. Classification depends on the relevant directions, the
    nature of the facility and the facts of the account. A model must
    not give a universal overdue-day threshold without checking the
    applicable current rule and any relevant exceptions. If a user asks
    whether a particular loan is an NPA, the answer may require
    account-level facts that are not available in a general document.

6.  PROVISIONING Provisioning recognises that some loan exposures may
    not be fully recovered. It affects reported profitability and the
    carrying value of assets. Provisioning requirements can vary by
    asset category, security, borrower, restructuring status and the
    applicable regulatory framework. A provision is not necessarily the
    same as a final write-off. A write-off is an accounting action and
    does not by itself mean that the borrower’s obligation has been
    legally extinguished.

7.  WHY ASSET-QUALITY METRICS NEED CONTEXT Gross NPA and net NPA
    measures are commonly used to describe aspects of a bank’s stressed
    advances. Their definitions and calculation details should be taken
    from the relevant reporting framework. A ratio should always be
    accompanied by its reporting date, denominator and source.
    Comparisons between institutions may be misleading if definitions,
    portfolios, reporting periods or accounting treatment differ. A
    single ratio does not explain the full credit-risk position.

8.  REGULATORY VERSION CONTROL Regulatory information changes. Before
    relying on a threshold, effective date, exemption or reporting
    requirement:

-   Identify the regulated entity type, such as a commercial bank, small
    finance bank, cooperative bank or NBFC.
-   Find the latest RBI direction or circular for that entity and topic.
-   Check the effective date, amendments and transitional provisions.
-   Identify the exact chapter, paragraph or section supporting the
    claim.
-   Distinguish binding regulatory text from explanatory articles or
    commentary.
-   If the source is unavailable or ambiguous, state the limitation
    rather than guessing.

9.  EXAMPLE OF A SAFE ANSWER Question: What overdue period makes a loan
    an NPA? A safe answer is: The applicable classification criteria
    depend on the type of regulated entity and facility and the current
    RBI directions. I would need to identify the institution and
    facility type and verify the latest applicable direction before
    giving an exact threshold. This avoids presenting a rule for one
    category as if it applied to all lenders.
