BANKING FUNDAMENTALS — INDIA Document type: Educational domain reference
Jurisdiction: India Prepared: 11 November 2026

PURPOSE AND SCOPE This document introduces common banking products,
terminology and processes. It is intended as domain material for testing
a financial question-answering system. It is not a bank’s product
disclosure, a current interest-rate schedule, or personalised financial
advice. Product conditions vary by institution, customer, date and
applicable regulation.

1.  WHAT A BANK DOES A bank accepts deposits, provides loans and payment
    services, and performs financial intermediation. It connects
    depositors who have funds with borrowers who need funds. Banks also
    support remittances, cards, account services, trade finance, cash
    management and digital payments. Banks must manage risks while
    meeting applicable legal and regulatory obligations.

2.  COMMON DEPOSIT PRODUCTS A savings deposit account is generally used
    by individuals for transactions and savings. Interest,
    minimum-balance conditions, charges and transaction limits depend on
    the bank’s terms and applicable rules. A current account is commonly
    used by businesses and entities with frequent transactions. Interest
    treatment, cash services, charges and limits depend on the bank’s
    terms. A fixed deposit, also called a term deposit, places money for
    a defined period under agreed conditions. The maturity amount
    depends on principal, rate, compounding convention and tenure.
    Premature withdrawal may be subject to conditions or penalties. A
    recurring deposit involves regular contributions over a chosen
    period. The maturity amount depends on the instalment schedule,
    tenure and applicable rate. A deposit customer should review the
    official product terms, premature-closure rules, tax treatment and
    deposit-insurance information before making a decision.

3.  LENDING TERMINOLOGY Principal is the original amount borrowed,
    excluding interest and charges. Interest is the cost of borrowing or
    return paid on a deposit, expressed as an amount or rate. Tenor or
    tenure is the length of time for which a loan or deposit runs. An
    EMI, or equated monthly instalment, is a periodic payment that
    typically includes principal and interest for an amortising loan.
    Collateral is an asset pledged or charged to support a loan. The
    lender’s rights over collateral depend on the contract and
    applicable law. Loan-to-value, or LTV, is the loan amount divided by
    the value of the financed asset, expressed as a percentage. A credit
    score is an indicator derived from credit-history information.
    Lenders may use it with income, repayment capacity, existing
    obligations, collateral and other underwriting factors.

4.  HOW LOAN REPAYMENT WORKS For a typical amortising loan, each
    scheduled payment covers interest due for the period and reduces
    outstanding principal. At the beginning of a standard repayment
    schedule, the interest portion may represent a larger share of the
    instalment; as principal declines, the interest portion generally
    declines too, assuming the rate and schedule remain unchanged. A
    floating-rate reset, missed payment, prepayment or restructuring can
    change the schedule. A borrower should consult the lender’s
    repayment statement for the actual outstanding balance and
    instalment details.

5.  ASSET QUALITY AND NON-PERFORMING ASSETS Asset quality describes the
    health of a lender’s loan portfolio. A non-performing asset, or NPA,
    is a loan or advance classified as non-performing under the
    applicable prudential rules. NPA classification, income recognition
    and provisioning must follow the current directions applicable to
    the regulated entity and facility. Do not assume one classification
    rule applies to every product or institution. An NPA ratio is a
    portfolio-level indicator and should be interpreted with its
    definition, reporting date and context.

6.  INTEREST RATES A lending rate may be fixed or floating. A fixed rate
    is intended to remain unchanged for the agreed period, subject to
    contract terms. A floating rate can change according to its
    benchmark, spread and reset terms. A rate change can affect the
    instalment amount, loan tenure, or both, depending on the contract
    and lender policy. A model must not invent a current policy rate,
    deposit rate or lending rate.

7.  BANKING PAYMENTS AND CUSTOMER PROTECTION Payment systems allow money
    to move between people, businesses and institutions. In India,
    digital payment methods include systems such as UPI, NEFT, RTGS and
    IMPS, each with its own operating rules and participant
    arrangements. Customers should verify beneficiary details, protect
    credentials, use official bank channels and report suspected
    unauthorised transactions promptly. A bank will have defined
    complaint and escalation channels; customers should consult the
    current official process.

8.  HOW TO ANSWER BANKING QUESTIONS RELIABLY First identify the product,
    institution, customer type, jurisdiction and date relevant to the
    question. Distinguish a general definition from a product-specific
    condition. If a question asks for an exact rate, fee, eligibility
    rule, overdue threshold or regulatory requirement, consult the
    latest official bank disclosure or applicable RBI direction. State
    the source and effective date. If the evidence is unavailable, say
    that the answer cannot be verified rather than guessing.
